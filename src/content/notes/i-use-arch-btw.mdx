## Introduction

"I use Arch, btw" is half joke, half identity. This note is the journey behind
mine: since 2024 I have been hopping distros on my ThinkPad, looking for the
one that fits. Three years later I can finally say what each stop taught me.

## The hopping years

- **Ubuntu (2024)** — the first Linux I ever installed. It worked, which is
  exactly what a first distro should do.
- **Linux Mint** — Ubuntu without the rough edges. The first time a desktop
  felt like mine.
- **Fedora** — newer software on a faster treadmill. I learned how much I
  notice when things move under me.
- **Debian** — the opposite lesson: nothing moves, and that is a feature.
- **Arch** — where the meme comes from. Installing it taught me more in one
  weekend than the previous year combined: partitions, bootloaders, and reading
  the wiki before asking.
- **Deepin OS** — the prettiest detour. Beautiful, unfamiliar, and a reminder
  that a desktop is a matter of taste.
- **Pop!_OS** — the pragmatic stop: gaming, NVIDIA, tiling, no drama.
- **EndeavourOS** — Arch without the installation exam. Comfortable enough
  that I almost stayed.
- **Linux Mint, again** — the loop closed. After chasing the newest and the
  hardest, I picked the one that stays out of my way.

## What Linux taught me

Before Linux I had never partitioned a disk, repaired a broken bootloader,
read a system log to solve my own problem, or lived in a terminal. Three years
of breaking things — usually my own system, on purpose or otherwise — taught me
all of it. The distro was never the point; the repairs were.
