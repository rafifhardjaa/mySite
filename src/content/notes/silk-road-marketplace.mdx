The first thing to unlearn about the Silk Road is that it was a road. It was never a single
route, and almost nobody ever walked its full length. It was a **network** — a shifting tangle
of caravan paths, mountain passes, oasis towns and sea lanes that opened, closed and rerouted
as empires rose and fell around them.

The name itself is late. Chinese sources called the routes the *Silk Routes* long before
anyone used the term; the popular singular comes from Ferdinand von Richthofen, a German
geographer, in 1877 — about thirteen centuries after the trade he was describing.

## Nobody Travelled the Whole Thing

Caravan trade worked by **relay**. A merchant in Samarkand might buy Chinese silk carried
by Sogdian traders from Kashgar, and sell it to a Levantine merchant who would carry it to
Antioch. Everyone took a slice. The person who finally loaded a bolt of silk onto a ship in
Canton had probably never met the person who sold it in the bazaar at Merv.

This is why the famous travellers are so misleading:

- **Zhang Qian**, the Han envoy sent west in the 2nd century BCE, is the closest thing to an
  originator — his missions opened diplomatic channels that turned trade into statecraft.
- **Xuanzang**, the Tang monk who walked to India and back in the early 7th century, was a
  pilgrim, not a merchant.
- **Ibn Battuta** travelled in the 14th century and his account is vivid, engaging, and
  widely suspected of serious exaggeration. Treat it as literature until you check it
  against other sources.
- **Marco Polo**, ditto — his numbers in particular do not survive scrutiny.

## What Actually Moved

The famous direction was silk west. It is genuinely remarkable that a material roughly as
common as linen in China became a diplomatic luxury in Rome, where Augustus reportedly
undressed a bolt to display it at a banquet.

But the traffic was far more two-way and far more varied than that:

- **West to East**: glassware, wool and linen, silver, horses, and the Roman taste for
  silver coinage.
- **East to West**: silk, porcelain, paper, tea, and lacquerware.
- **Into the middle**: spices from India, cotton from the Indus, precious stones from
  Afghanistan, and Rhodian wine pressed at the far end.

One material quietly reorganised an industry: **paper**. It is traditionally held that
papermakers were captured after the Battle of Talas in 751 CE and carried to Samarkand, where
paper-making spread west and eventually into Europe. Even if that specific story is
complicated, paper's journey from China through Samarkand to Baghdad and then to Europe is
not in doubt, and it changed how knowledge was stored and copied.

## The People Who Actually Did It

If one group deserves the credit for the Silk Road, it is the **Sogdians** — Iranian-speaking
trader communities with diaspora colonies strung across the network, from Samarkand to
northern China. They spoke the languages, carried the credit, and absorbed the risk. They are
far less famous than the travellers above, and they were the actual infrastructure.

## Why It Ended

The routes did not stop so much as they got outcompeted. As the fifteenth century turned:

- The Black Death moved along these same corridors, and the disruption was severe.
- The Mongol successor states in Central Asia fractured, raising the cost and risk of the
  northern routes.
- **1453**: Constantinople falls, and the old overland endpoints lose their main market.
- Maritime routes widen the world: da Gama reaches India by sea in 1498.

By then, silk was travelling by ship, and the geography had been quietly rewritten.

## Why It Is Still Worth Studying

Because the road is a good excuse to talk about how trade actually works. Prices, credit,
contract enforcement, standardised weights, and the exchange of ideas that ride along with
goods — religions, technologies, artistic styles, and diseases, both directions.

The through-line running through all of it is that **connectivity is an infrastructure
problem, not an inevitability**. The routes worked because states kept them safe enough to
be worth the risk, and they degraded when that guarantee weakened.
